    Core settings and how this works

    Output voltage follows the turns ratio: Vout = Vin × Nsecond ÷ Nfirst. It does not matter which coil you treat as the input, so long as you connect the source to the coil you wound with the right turn count. The mod caps the total turns of both coils at 60 (small) or 240 (medium), and each coil needs at least 1 turn. One copper wire item is used per turn. The values above are the mod’s default server config, so change them if your server does. They only affect the resistance estimates, never the ratio.

    In the mod each coil’s inductance is turns² × Al. The goggle readout shows resistances: the magnetizing (parallel) branch is K × L × multiplier of the coil with fewer turns, and the stray branches come from L × (1 − K). Higher turn counts give a higher parallel resistance, which means less idle current. A 1:1 winding can also split the electrical networks apart if the server has splitting transformers enabled. Output voltage is the ideal no-load figure, and load current will pull it down through the stray resistance.
